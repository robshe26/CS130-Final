# The Cost of Extra Traction: Front-Wheel Drive vs. The World 

Living in Buffalo comes with many advantage: the sports, the friendliness, and the community. However, living in this area also comes with its disadvantages, namely the weather. Buffalo is home to some of the worst winter weather, being ranked 4th snowiest city in America by Fox Weather. When it comes to driving in the winter, this makes it pretty difficult facing ice, snow, and blizzards. To combat this people buy Four-Wheel Drive and All-Wheel Drive vehicles for extra secuirty. Even dealerships heavily market these systems as absolute necessitites for winter survival. Consumers gladly check the box for the AWD upgrade, viewing it as a ital safety feature for those snowy days. 

However, these additional spinning wheel require a sacrafice. Sending power to all four wheels, rather than just two, requires heavy drivetrains and an engine that is forced to work harder. By analyzing the data of over 5,000 vehicles,by Austin Cody Bart, we began to uncover what this extra traction is costing us at the pump every day.

### Is the MPG penalty worth it?

This additional power, and the traction that comes with four wheels moving makes them advantageuous in the winter time. This begs the question: _is the tradeoff in gas miliage worth the extra safety in the winter?_

For most people, I feel the answer to this would be no. While it is helpful, in my experience driving a Front-Wheel drive car in the winter has not been an issue. For unexperienced drivers in winter weather it might be advantageous, but for people that live in Buffalo and regularly drive in the snow, a Front-Wheel drive is enough. More tires moving on the road does not give you extra stopping power, in fact it might make it worse becuase of the added weight. The roads are mostly plowed, and serious weather events only happen a select few number of times a year. So, in my case the tradeoff between the extra money in gas, versus the extra traction is not worth it

__For others it may come down to your specific reality:__
- __The Need:__ If you are a first responder, a plow driver, or live on top of a big hill, the ability to gain extra traction and acceleration is a must have. For these indiviuals the "traction tax" may be worth it for the added piece of mind.
- __The Average Commuter:__ For the average commuter who is driving on treated roads and cant afford to wait untill the plows come or a whiteout passes, the worth factor changes. Paying for a lower fuel economy 365 days a year to solve a traction problem that only exists a few days a year is a poor return on investment.
